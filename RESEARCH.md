## Methodology and integrity rules

- **Source types:** `primary` (official team, league, publisher, brand kit), `database` (structured esports database), `secondary` (credible reporting), `social` (official social account), `community` (community-maintained dataset), `unknown`.
- **Confidence:** `verified` (2+ independent sources agree), `single-source`, `uncertain`, `unknown`. `unknown` means we must not publish a value.
- Important claims are cross-checked where sources exist. Conflicts are recorded with both versions and their URLs; they are **not** silently resolved.
- No statistic, biography, result, date, placement, achievement or quote is invented to complete a page. Missing information stays explicitly unknown.
- Synthetic portraits, hatched bars and placeholder strings in `.impeccable/mocks/decision/` are **design material only** and are never treated as evidence.
- A brand kit or official social account is evidence that an asset exists, **not** a license to reuse it or a claim of endorsement.

## Verified team and player information

Retrieved 2026-11-17. Source key: **PO** primary/official, **DB** database/wiki, **SEC** secondary news, **INT** interview. Confidence: **V** = 2+ sources, **S** = single-source.

**Verified (`V`):**
- Team Liquid ID (TLID) is Team Liquid's Indonesian **Mobile Legends: Bang Bang** squad, with Main and Academy rosters; the official page describes it as holding it down for Team Liquid in Indonesia. [S1, S2]
- Parent organization: Team Liquid, founded in the Netherlands in 2000, co-CEOs Victor Goossens and Steve Arhancet. [S3, S2]
- **21 May 2024:** Team Liquid announced the acquisition of Southeast Asian org STUN.GG and its properties AURA Esports (Indonesia) and ECHO Esports (Philippines). Financial terms were not disclosed; existing staff and players joined Team Liquid. Described as the first SEA mobile esports organizations acquired by a global organization. [S4, S5, S6, S7, S20]
- Rebrand path: competed as **Liquid AURA** during MPL ID S13 / EWC 2024, then fully as **Team Liquid ID** from MPL Indonesia S14; the Philippine counterpart is Team Liquid PH. [S2, S7, S8, S9]
- Predecessor: AURA Esports, an Indonesian multi-game organization whose MLBB team was **AURA Fire** (also "AURA Dirga" in Indonesian Wikipedia), in MPL-ID since roughly Season 3. [S11, S10, S12]
- League: MPL Indonesia, the franchised tier-one MLBB league (9 teams, two seasons per year), with an MDL Indonesia academy pathway. [S9, S2, S19]

**Single-source / uncertain (`S`):** "Kuda Biru" nickname, "Cavalries" fan club and Samsung partnership come only from Indonesian Wikipedia's infobox; "Team Liquid Aqua" as the MDL-ID academy name appears only in a Liquipedia navbox; an Esports Charts claim that AURA was the world's 6th most-watched organization of 2023 is quoted by secondary outlets. None should be published as fact without cross-checking.

**Conflicts recorded, not resolved:**
1. **AURA founding year:** 2017 by Dirga Putra Utama [S10 text] vs June 2018 / MLBB team on 2 Aug 2018 [S11, S12, S7] vs 2024 / 21 May 2024 [S10 infobox, S19, which is the rebrand date]. No single founding year can be asserted.
2. **S13 team name:** "Liquid AURA" / "Liquid Aura" [S4, S6] vs "AURA Liquid" [S9]. This reinforces the S13 attribution conflict recorded in the tournament section.
3. **M6 final date:** 15 Dec 2024 [S13, S15] vs Moonton's 14 December [S14]. Minor.

**Unknowns:** acquisition price, current legal ownership structure, exact AURA founding date, and the exact first date the team competed under the TLID name.

**Strong enough to build with now (with citations and as-of dates):**

**Populate with caveats:**

- Hero pick/ban/win-rate tables: single-source per page and some direct fetches were blocked; useful only with a visible source and verification date.
- Aggregate W/L records: agree across Liquipedia and Esports Charts, but win-rate percentages disagree because scopes differ.

**Do not populate yet:**
- Real player portraits or logos until permission is clarified. The existing synthetic portraits and hatched placeholders in the mockups are design material and must remain labeled illustrative.
- Any statistic whose season, scope or source cannot be shown. Use "to be verified" rather than a plausible number.
- Founding date, legal structure, sponsorship claims, fan-club names, or "Kuda Biru" nickname — all single-source or conflicting.
- Post-S18 fixtures: none found.

**Recommended data architecture for the eventual build:** a hand-curated, cited, dated JSON file in the repo as the single source of truth; no runtime third-party calls; if automation is added later, a weekly build-time sync in CI using a token held in CI secrets, with Liquipedia API terms respected and attribution carried through. Avoid HTML scraping, reverse-engineered Moonton endpoints and client-side tokens.

**Bottom line:** a credible, well-attributed site is feasible with public data for identity, roster, tournament history and a limited set of player statistics. Depth beyond that is limited by stale official pages, source conflicts and licensing uncertainty — so the product must treat "unknown" as a first-class state rather than filling gaps with invented values.
